# Grimm Dominion: Decision register

**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-11
**Authority:** latest explicit creator decisions > approved production specifications > existing implementation > exploratory GDD.

## Confirmed decisions (creator approval, 2026-11-11)

| ID | Decision | Confirmed outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D-001 | Human team composition | **4 Dark Heroes vs. 4 Light Heroes** (eight player slots) |
| D-002 | Dark Hero experience | **Hybrid:** each is a physical playable character **and** commands autonomous monsters from an RTS-level perspective |
| D-003 | Light Hero experience | **Switchable or hybrid perspective/control.** Exact camera modes, transition behavior, and controls are not yet specified |
| D-004 | First delivery platform | **PC browser first.** Other platforms remain future possibilities, not requirements for the first build |
| D-005 | Core match objective | Light Heroes gather power and assault the central castle to kill the Dark Lord. Dark Heroes hunt/stop them before they succeed. Exact elimination and timer rules are unresolved |
| D-006 | Bots | **Permanent solo/co-op support**, plus filling vacant player slots; bots are part of the product, not development-only |
| D-007 | Development workflow | **Claude Code with direct GitHub repository access.** The repository is the single source of truth |
| D-008 | Visual identity | **Classic Warcraft III-inspired original 3D**, exaggerated silhouettes, clear camera readability, painterly gothic fairytale environments. Do not copy protected assets |
| D-009 | Product ambition | Develop the **full game cumulatively**, using narrow verified playable milestones rather than disposable prototypes |
| D-010 | Core gameplay identity | Autonomous dark-side monster armies, incomplete-information hunting/stealth, differentiated Light Hero progression, castle assault |
| D-011 | Engine constraint | **Do not use Godot** for the principal implementation |
| D-012 | Dark-team scouting information | **Shared confirmed alerts, private live vision:** when one Dark Hero's Scout confirms a Light Hero sighting, all four Dark Heroes receive the sighting alert. Only the Scout's owner receives its live vision. An alert is a time-stamped observation, not continuous tracking or team-wide shared vision |
| D-013 | Light Hero stealth foundation | **Universal sight, cover and detection rules for all Light Heroes**, with character-specific modifiers and abilities. Exact per-character differences and tuning values are not yet approved |
| D-014 | Detection feedback | **Environmental and audio cues plus a subtle local attention indicator** for the Light Hero when a monster is noticing them. Exact presentation, timing and tuning remain to be playtested. Do not use this UI to reveal unseen enemy positions |
| D-015 | Dark Lord's core role | **Dormant ruler and ultimate castle objective/final boss.** The four playable Dark Heroes defend him and the castle. The creator finds an active AI-controlled Dark Lord concept appealing, but **has not approved it as a mechanic** |
| D-016 | Awakening outcome | **Awakening begins a dangerous final confrontation, not automatic Dark victory.** The awakened Dark Lord fights alongside the four Dark Heroes against surviving Light Heroes. Exact boss combat mechanics and terminal victory conditions are unresolved |
| D-017 | Dark Energy awakening race | The Dark Lord has a **tiered Dark Energy meter** that fills naturally over time. Individual evil deeds by Dark Heroes **or their minions** each grant a **small, immediate, one-time addition of Dark Energy**. **Evil deeds do not increase the natural accumulation rate.** Exact thresholds, rewards and tuning remain open |
| D-018 | Light Heroes' early-win route | Light Heroes may **breach the Dark Lord's sanctum and slay him before awakening**, ending the conflict in Light victory. If he awakens first, the Light Heroes instead face the awakened Dark Lord **and** the Dark Heroes in a dangerous final battle. Exact vulnerability/defenses of dormant Dark Lord are unresolved |
| D-019 | Light-side awakening sabotage | **Light Heroes can sabotage corruption sources/Dark operations** to slow the **passive Dark Energy generation rate**, without directly subtracting already accumulated energy. Details of eligible targets, restoration and costs remain open |
| D-020 | Corruption source acceleration and correction | **Corruption sources, not individual evil deeds, increase the natural accumulation rate of Dark Energy.** Their active contribution is the strategic target for Light sabotage. This explicitly supersedes the earlier reading of D-017 that every deed permanently increased energy-per-time; source creation, stacking, maintenance, and exact rates remain unapproved |
| D-021 | Two kinds of corruption source | **Hybrid sources:** Dark Heroes can corrupt existing world landmarks for major passive Dark Energy production **and** construct smaller corruption sources at strategically chosen positions. Both raise passive generation while contributing. Landmark lists, construction mechanics, placement limits, rates, maintenance and sabotage interactions remain open |
| D-022 | Light sanctuaries from purified landmarks | When Light Heroes purify a **major corrupted landmark**, it becomes a **Light-controlled sanctuary**, not merely a neutral site. This creates recapturable territory for both teams. Sanctuary benefits, capture/re-corruption process, ownership visibility and costs remain unresolved; this does not approve analogous conversion of small constructed corruption sources |
| D-023 | Sanctuary function and vulnerability | **Hybrid Light sanctuaries** provide **recovery and character progression opportunities**, including healing/regrouping and ways to develop power. They are **not completely safe**: Dark Heroes must be able to threaten and eventually recapture them. Exact services, progression economy, defensive mechanics, capture process and safety limitations remain unapproved |
| D-024 | Dark recapture of Light sanctuaries | **Hybrid conquest:** Dark Heroes can send their autonomous minions to assault a sanctuary and **begin re-corrupting it**, while a Dark Hero's **physical participation in the ritual makes the process more effective**. Light Heroes can intervene to defend the site. Exact channel/contest timing, defenders, interruption rules and amount of the hero's advantage remain unresolved |
| D-025 | Sanctuary defense model | **Baseline natural protective wards/defenses plus Light Hero investment in upgrades, rituals or character abilities.** Unupgraded defenses must delay or complicate a Dark assault, not defeat a serious army unaided. Sanctuaries remain vulnerable to Dark Hero/minion attack and recapture. Exact protections, upgrades, costs, control interactions and strength remain unresolved |
| D-026 | Two-stage sanctuary siege | **Dark recapture requires two distinct stages:** Dark minions/armies first **weaken or breach the sanctuary's protective wards**, then initiate a **corruption ritual** to convert the sanctuary. Light Heroes can defend wards, interrupt the ritual, or counterattack to retake control. A Dark Hero can physically join the ritual to improve it (D-024), but is not required. Ward health, breach conditions, ritual timing, interruption/reset and recapture/repair rules remain unapproved |

| D-027 | Sanctuary siege recovery | **Hybrid recovery after an interrupted siege:** ward damage **persists until repaired by Light Heroes**. Corruption ritual progress **gradually decays when Dark forces lose control** of the sanctuary. Repeated attacks can leave lasting ward damage, but abandoned ritual progress does not remain indefinitely. Exact control definition, repair requirements and decay tuning remain unresolved |
| D-028 | Resource-backed hybrid ward restoration | **Sanctuary ward repairs consume resources and can be performed through established restoration rituals that continue operating while Light Heroes explore elsewhere.** A Light Hero's personal participation **accelerates restoration** but is not required after the ritual is established. Exact resource sources, cost, restoration speed, ritual upkeep, interruptions, player abilities, and when rituals can be established remain unresolved |
| D-029 | Hybrid Dark disruption of sanctuary restoration | **Dark pressure slows the rate of an active sanctuary restoration ritual, while destroying its physical ritual anchor stops restoration entirely.** Light Heroes can defend the anchor or drive attackers away to relieve pressure. Pressure conditions, anchor visibility/health, whether a halted ritual needs replacement or restart, and loss/recovery of invested resources are unresolved |
| D-030 | Layered Light sanctuary threat awareness | **All Light Heroes receive a shared alert when a sanctuary is threatened and can see that sanctuary's ward condition.** Heroes near the sanctuary receive more detailed local visual and audio information. **Distant Light Heroes do not automatically learn attacking units' identities or precise positions** from the shared alert. Trigger thresholds, severity levels, map presentation, and exact definition of nearby remain unresolved |
| D-031 | Escalating sanctuary threat notifications | **Two levels of alert:** all Light Heroes first get a **subtle sanctuary warning when an attack begins**, which **escalates to an urgent warning upon ward breach or the start of the Dark corruption ritual**. Preserve D-030: shared alerts report sanctuary condition, not unseen attacker identities, counts, or exact positions. Initial attack definition, repeat/cooldown rules, presentation and urgency tuning remain unresolved |
| D-032 | Light Hero body-and-ghost revival | On Light Hero death, the player retains **a physical body at the death location and a controllable ghost** rather than simply leaving the match. They can be **revived at their body**, or **travel as a ghost to an uncorrupted sanctuary and revive there**. A **church in a village** is a possible additional revival location, **not yet approved**. Who revives the body, what the ghost can do or perceive, whether enemy forces can affect either, costs/limits, revival positioning and any endgame consequences are unresolved |
| D-033 | Limited spiritual ghost interactions | **Ghosts remain playable while dead** and can navigate, perceive spiritual landmarks, and perform **a limited set of meaningful supernatural interactions**. They **cannot freely reveal hidden enemy positions** or bypass established hunting/fog-of-war restrictions through unrestricted scouting. Exact spirit interactions, senses, ghost movement, visibility to enemies, danger and constraints require later approval. This does not grant combat, physical-world manipulation or tactical enemy vision by default |
| D-034 | Body revival requires living Light Hero rescue | **Reviving a fallen Light Hero at their physical body requires another living Light Hero to reach and revive them.** The dead player's ghost cannot independently revive at its body. Their independent alternative is traveling as a ghost to an eligible uncorrupted sanctuary (D-032). Revival interaction timing, resources, channel interruptions and what bots can do remain unresolved; this does not approve ghost self-revival at the body |
| D-035 | Ghost pilgrimage spiritual obstacles | **Ghosts encounter spiritual obstacles while traveling to revive**, including supernatural barriers, corrupted regions, and environmental puzzles. These complicate navigation but **do not permanently eliminate the ghost**. The ghost journey is interactive rather than an automatic or completely safe teleport, without requiring a second full lethal hunting loop. Exact obstacle mechanics, frequency, duration, solutions and consequences of failure remain unapproved |
| D-036 | Ghost perspective shares physical kingdom | **Ghosts inhabit the same physical kingdom map as living players**, but perceive a transformed spiritual layer with hidden spirits, supernatural markings and ethereal pathways invisible to living Heroes. This is **not a separate realm or wholly different geography**. Specific spirit entities, path traversal rules and information visibility remain unapproved; spiritual perception does not grant free enemy scouting (D-033) |
| D-037 | Hybrid ghost-only pathway traversal | **Some ethereal paths are immediately traversable by ghosts, while others must be revealed or unlocked through limited spiritual interactions or environmental puzzles.** Navigation should combine discovery with occasional short interactions, not mandatory lengthy puzzles on every route. Exact interactions, path layout, frequency, unlock persistence and consequences of blocked routes remain unapproved. Ghost travel must remain a viable non-permanent-elimination revival option (D-032/D-035) |
| D-038 | Sanctuary ghost resurrection ritual | **A ghost reaching an eligible uncorrupted sanctuary must perform a short spiritual resurrection ritual to return to life there.** Arrival alone does not automatically resurrect the hero. Ritual duration, interaction design, whether enemies or corruption can interrupt it, and consequences of interruption remain unapproved. No additional resource sacrifice or temporary weakness is approved by this choice |
| D-039 | Sanctuary resurrection eligibility during a siege | **A ghost may perform a resurrection ritual at a sanctuary under Dark attack, until that sanctuary is fully corrupted.** Once the Dark side completes its takeover, resurrection there becomes unavailable and the ghost must travel to a different eligible sanctuary. Ghosts can spiritually sense the sanctuary's corruption/eligibility change before arriving, but this must not reveal hidden enemy positions or identities. Exact alert presentation, ritual failure behavior during a takeover, and alternate-destination cases remain unresolved |
| D-040 | Death-age-scaled sanctuary resurrection ritual | **Resurrection rituals are longer for recently deceased Light Heroes and become shorter the longer that hero has been dead.** This is intended to prevent quick respawns during an active siege while avoiding unnecessary delay for long-dead players. The scaling curve, minimum and maximum ritual times, when the duration locks, any siege-specific rules, and interruptions remain unresolved. The choice does not authorize instant resurrection, a fixed duration, or additional resource costs |
| D-041 | Resurrection interruption during sanctuary siege | **Only full corruption of the sanctuary prevents or ends a ghost's resurrection there.** Ordinary nearby combat does not interrupt the resurrection ritual, and there is **no separate Dark spiritual-interference action** that can disrupt it. If the sanctuary is fully corrupted before resurrection completes, that ghost must find another eligible sanctuary (D-039). Numeric ritual timing and presentation remain undecided. **This B decision explicitly supersedes the previously recorded D-041 targeted-interference concept** |
| D-042 | Hybrid Light Hero progression | **All Light Heroes share a common foundational advancement layer while each playable Light Hero also has a distinctive character-specific progression system.** Characters may advance through different activities and resources, while preserving common learnable progression expectations. The shared layer's form (levels, equipment, milestones, currencies), each character's identity, exact rewards, and balance rules remain **unapproved**. This decision does not establish generic XP grinding or any specific class mechanics |
| D-043 | Shared Light progression through equipment and resources | **All Light Heroes can acquire equipment and resources to develop their foundational capabilities**, while character-specific progression gives them distinct powers and uses for what they acquire. This does not authorize a universal XP/level bar or prescribe identical equipment slots for every character. Acquisition sources, inventories, crafting/upgrading, trade, loss on death, scarcity, class-specific interactions and economy balance remain unapproved |
| D-044 | Hybrid Light resource acquisition | **Light Heroes obtain equipment and resources through both exploration/discovery across the kingdom and completion of meaningful world objectives.** Exploration supplies useful finds; objectives such as defending settlements, reclaiming landmarks or confronting threats provide more substantial rewards. Character identities can encourage different destinations and activities. Exact resources, reward amounts, locations, respawning, objective types, loot ownership and anti-farming systems remain unapproved |
| D-045 | Hybrid Light equipment upgrading | **Light Heroes can change equipment and perform basic crafting or minor improvements while exploring**, but **major upgrades require an appropriate specialized location**, such as a sanctuary or workshop. This separates routine preparation from significant advancement while giving territory and settlements strategic value. Exact inventory/equipment changes, crafting tiers, facility requirements, costs, upgrade categories, and whether actions can occur under threat remain unapproved |
| D-046 | Hybrid Light resource ownership | **Personal equipment, special ingredients and character-specific materials belong to individual Light Heroes**, while **common resources earned through major world objectives enter a shared team pool** available for collective advancement. Which items can be traded, shared spending rights or approval rules, resource denominations, caps and anti-grief protections remain unresolved. This does not imply all personally discovered resources become communal or all team resources can be spent without restriction |
| D-047 | Shared pool spending permissions and purchase-abuse protection | **Hybrid spending confirmed:** Light Heroes can make small purchases using shared resources without a team-wide approval each time, while major team-funded investments require collective authorization. **There must also be a protection against one player making excessive small purchases and exhausting the shared pool.** The creator proposed tracking each player's contributions so they can allocate earned spending rights toward either personal minor purchases or a larger collective purchase. **Contribution-weighted spending credits are a promising PROPOSAL, not yet a confirmed formula or sole approved mechanism.** Define how contributions are counted, shared participation, budgets, approval mechanics, and safeguards before implementing |
| D-048 | Hybrid allocation of team spending rights | **All Light Heroes receive a baseline spending allowance from shared team successes, with additional spending rights awarded for meaningful individual contributions.** These allowances permit limited individual small purchases or may be **voluntarily pledged toward a major team purchase**. Allowances represent permission to spend from the real shared resource pool, **not duplicated currency**; team resources are deducted once on purchase. Contribution evaluation must account for indirect support, reconnaissance and defense rather than only direct kills. Exact allocation ratios, contribution events, anti-farming, spend caps, refresh cadence, and major purchase approval mechanics are unresolved |
| D-049 | Pledge-funded major Light team investments | **Major team purchases are authorized by voluntary pledges of players' spending allowances, without an additional team vote.** A proposed major investment proceeds automatically once sufficient allowance is pledged **and** the actual shared resource pool can afford it. Pledging represents a player's consent to allocate their limited spending rights, not new resources. Safeguards must prevent the same allowance or shared resources being spent twice. Pledge withdrawal/cancellation, concurrency, reservation behavior, shortfalls, purchase thresholds and proposal limits remain unresolved |
| D-050 | Forest Witch one-time shrine pilgrimage | **All Light Heroes can purify and reclaim corrupted major landmarks.** The **Forest Witch additionally performs a unique ley-line ritual at each eligible shrine to increase her own power**, and **each shrine can grant her ritual progression only once per match**. She must journey to different shrines across the kingdom to keep advancing through this shrine-based loop, rather than repeatedly farming one site; visiting every shrine is the route to exhausting those unique shrine rewards. General landmark purification is not exclusive to the Witch. Which landmarks qualify as her shrines, ritual prerequisites, whether the reward is fixed/selected, exact power increments, and the effects of later re-corruption remain unapproved. A repeated ritual at the same shrine cannot grant another progression reward |
| D-051 | Forest Witch shrine-themed ritual rewards | **Each eligible shrine's one-time Forest Witch ritual grants Arcane Power AND a choice among shrine-themed magical abilities.** The choices offered are associated with that particular shrine, so the Witch's build emerges from her pilgrimage and selections rather than a single generic spell list or a fixed spell assignment. Preserve D-050's **once-per-unique-shrine-per-match** reward limit. Exact shrine list, offered abilities, number of options, Arcane Power amounts/uses, reroll rules and selection timing are unapproved; no second reward may be earned by re-corrupting and re-purifying the same shrine |
| D-052 | Witch ritual requires prior shrine purification | **A shrine must already be purified and under Light control before the Forest Witch may perform her personal one-time ley-line progression ritual there.** Purification and Witch advancement are **two distinct actions**. Any Light Hero, **including the Witch**, can perform the ordinary purification step (D-050), but the Witch's personal ritual does **not** itself cleanse a corrupted shrine. Once purified, the Witch can perform her unique ritual there if she has not claimed that shrine's one-time reward. If the site becomes fully corrupted again before completion, it is no longer eligible until purified again; detailed in-progress ritual interruption/resumption is unresolved. Exact channel timing, eligibility under siege, progress rules, and discovery/navigation methods remain open |
| D-053 | Forest Witch supernatural shrine discovery | **The Forest Witch discovers shrines through supernatural intuition rather than receiving their exact locations upfront.** She perceives distant magical disturbances; these sensory indications grow more precise as she approaches a shrine, and environmental clues help her interpret the signals and navigate. Shrine discovery remains experiential exploration, **not automatic global map markers or perfect knowledge of shrine coordinates**. This sense provides no privileged information about unseen Dark Heroes or minions. Exact visual/audio/haptic presentation, sensing range, whether it is passive or requires an active action, and interactions with terrain, concealment or corruption remain unapproved |
| D-054 | Witch passive and focused shrine sensing | **The Forest Witch receives subtle passive supernatural/environmental clues during ordinary exploration, and can intentionally focus her senses for clearer shrine guidance.** Focused sensing clarifies the disturbance and helps interpret the direction or proximity of a shrine without revealing exact shrine coordinates, automatic destination pins or unseen enemy positions. Passive cues do not require repeated button presses. Whether focused sensing consumes a resource, slows or exposes the Witch, its range, duration, cooldown, and visual/audio accessibility treatment remain unapproved |
| D-055 | Focused Witch sensing creates detectable magical disturbance | **When the Forest Witch uses focused shrine sensing, she creates a supernatural disturbance that nearby Dark Scouts may detect.** This is the chosen primary risk of focused sensing, rather than the previously proposed reduced-mobility/awareness penalty. Detecting a magical disturbance provides **evidence to investigate**, not automatic visual confirmation of the Witch or continuous tracking; D-012's time-stamped confirmed-sighting alerts and private Scout-owner live vision remain intact. Passive shrine intuition does not automatically broadcast the same focused-sensing disturbance. Exact event strength, radius, detection conditions, Scout reaction/reporting, Witch feedback, duration, and counterplay remain unresolved |
| D-056 | Private approximate Witch magic-disturbance report | **When a Dark Scout detects the Forest Witch's focused-sensing disturbance, only that Scout's owning Dark Hero receives an approximate report of magical activity in the observed area.** The report is a time-stamped, uncertain clue, not the Witch's exact/current position, not confirmed identification or a confirmed Light Hero sighting, and **not a team-wide notification**. The Scout's normal investigation and any later confirmed sighting follow existing perception rules (D-012/D-013). Other Dark Heroes learn of a confirmed Hero sighting only through the separately authorized shared sighting alert. Report uncertainty, expiry, map/UI presentation, Scout detection limits and possible player-initiated communication remain unresolved |
| D-057 | Forest Witch shrine ritual resets fully on interruption | **If the Forest Witch takes damage or her personal shrine progression ritual is disrupted while channeling, that attempt is cancelled and its progress resets completely.** To claim the shrine's one-time Arcane Power and themed ability choice she must **restart the ritual from the beginning** at an eligible, purified shrine. Interrupted attempts do not consume the shrine's one-time reward; only a successfully completed rite counts for D-050/D-051. Full Dark corruption makes the shrine ineligible until purified again (D-052). Exact ritual duration, interruptions other than damage, channel/movement restrictions, notification and any defensive counterplay remain unresolved. This rule is **specific to the Witch's progression ritual**, not ghost resurrection (D-041) or sanctuary restoration (D-027/D-029) |
| D-058 | Witch shrine ritual emits locally detectable magical activity | **Performing the Forest Witch's one-time shrine progression ritual generates supernatural visual/environmental effects and a local magical disturbance that nearby Dark Scouts can detect and investigate.** Detection of ritual activity does **not** automatically identify or precisely locate the Witch, count as a confirmed Light Hero sighting, or issue a kingdom-wide/team-wide Dark alert. The ordinary confirmed-sighting policy (D-012) continues to apply independently. This is a distinct action from the Witch's focused shrine sensing (D-055/D-056); whether detecting ritual magic produces the same Scout-owner private approximate report, and the precise range, duration, strength, sound/visual cues, and counterplay, remain unapproved |
| D-059 | Forest Witch shrine progression is an interactive ritual | **The Witch's once-per-shrine progression rite requires active ritual interactions**, such as manipulating magical symbols or interacting with ritual points around the shrine, **rather than passive stationary channeling or mere movement within a circle**. The Witch must manage those interactions while vulnerable to interruption. Under D-057, taking damage or an interruption cancels the attempt and **fully resets ritual progress**, without consuming the one-time shrine reward. The user's later selection of **C supersedes the immediately prior B selection** (movement within a ritual circle), which was not yet added to the decision register. Exact interaction controls, number/order of steps, movement or combat permission, ritual pacing, how damage/failed manipulation triggers interruption, and accessibility safeguards are unapproved |
| D-060 | Forest Witch rituals share fundamentals with shrine-specific twists | **All eligible Forest Witch shrine progression rituals use the same learnable foundation of magical interactions.** Each individual shrine adds a **distinctive challenge or variation tied to its own nature and the abilities it can grant**, rather than being an identical reskin or an entirely separate unlearnable minigame. The purpose is for the player to develop mastery of witchcraft while discovering different ritual challenges on the pilgrimage. Keep D-059's active interaction, D-057's full reset on interruption, and D-050's one-time per-shrine reward. Exact shared interaction verbs, shrine-specific variations, difficulty progression, counts, input/UI requirements, and timing remain open; approving this pattern does **not** approve any example puzzle or mechanic |
| D-061 | Forest Witch ritual interaction: directing magical currents | **The shared physical interaction for Forest Witch shrine rituals is to direct magical energy between ritual points, establishing and manipulating connections and redirecting currents through the shrine to complete its magical configuration.** Distinct shrines can vary the arrangement and properties of this energy-flow challenge under D-060, while using familiar core actions. This replaces previously proposed rune-only alignment or unstructured sequences as the central ritual interaction. Preserve active participation (D-059), interruption full reset (D-057), one-time ritual rewards (D-050/D-051), and local magical exposure (D-058). Exact controls, whether energy flows continuously or in steps, nodes/paths, rules of redirection, success conditions, feedback, input affordances, and accessibility remain unapproved |
| D-062 | Hybrid physical apparatus and direct current manipulation for Witch shrine rites | **The Witch's shrine ritual interaction system combines hands-on manipulation of physical shrine apparatus (such as movable or orientable ritual objects that change pathways) AND directly guiding or connecting magical currents among that apparatus and ritual points.** The two interaction modes should serve complementary functions within the same embodied ritual, not act as two unrelated minigames. Under D-060, shrines may vary how these modes appear and interact. This does **not** require identical counts or ordering of both interactions at every shrine, or approve any particular object, gesture, puzzle, UI, control scheme or 3D implementation. Preserve D-059's active play, D-057's full reset on interruption, D-058's locally detectable magic and D-050/D-051's one-time rewards. Exact freeform vs anchor-targeted guidance, input affordances, interaction flow, combat/movement rules and accessibility remain unapproved |
| D-063 | Goblin Outlaw personal gold with shared objective resources | **The Goblin Outlaw personally owns gold earned through his theft and individual acquisition activities**, preserving his GDD gold-collection and hidden-tavern upgrade progression. **Major world objectives still generate common resources for the Light team shared pool**, under D-044/D-046, rather than all rewards being diverted into the Goblin's private economy. His personal stolen gold is distinct from the shared pool and must not be silently pooled or counted twice as team currency. How monster drops, personal contributions to objectives, trade, gold spending outside the tavern, resource conversion and reward amounts work remains unapproved. This confirms option B, not an entirely isolated Goblin economy or a universal team-gold model |
| D-064 | Temporary Dark occupation and Goblin-led tavern counterattack | **Dark forces can discover and temporarily occupy the Goblin Outlaw's hidden tavern, denying its upgrade services while occupied.** Occupation is **reversible**, not permanent destruction and not automatic relocation. The Goblin can **sneak into the occupied tavern, activate its brutal built-in defenses, and lead a counterattack alongside the tavern's hiding denizens** to retake it. Light allies may also help reclaim the tavern, as in the selected option B, but exact roles remain open. The defenses and denizens make the tavern an active, defensible location rather than only a shop; this does not imply a guaranteed win or instant clearance after activation. **Unapproved:** how the tavern is found or occupied, which defenses exist and how to access them, whether denizens need rescue/rallying, their AI/combat abilities, occupation duration, recapture victory conditions, repair costs, ownership, respawns, and whether Dark forces can exploit the defenses. Preserve D-063's distinct personal-gold economy |
| D-065 | Secret master mechanism triggers Goblin tavern uprising | **To initiate recapture of an occupied Secret Tavern, the Goblin can infiltrate it and reach a concealed master mechanism. Activating that mechanism turns on the tavern's brutal built-in defenses and signals the hiding tavern denizens to join a coordinated counterattack.** This is a single concealed activation point, not a required multi-switch preparation sequence or chain of activations. The mechanism starts the uprising but does **not** automatically remove Dark occupiers or guarantee victory; a battle for recapture follows D-064. Exact concealment and accessibility, whether Dark occupants can find, block or disable the mechanism, timing/defense behavior, denizen AI/coordination, and what happens if the counterattack fails are still open |
| D-066 | Dark Heroes can discover and guard the tavern's master mechanism | **Dark Heroes who occupy the Goblin's secret tavern can discover the concealed master uprising mechanism and position defenders to guard it, but cannot destroy it.** The Goblin must use stealth, misdirection or a coordinated assault to get to and activate the mechanism despite any defenders. Finding the mechanism is not an automatic reveal on occupation, and guarding it does not grant omniscient knowledge of the Goblin. The earlier suggestion that Dark forces could sabotage or disable the mechanism was **not selected**. Activation still initiates the denizen-assisted defense uprising (D-065), not an automatic tavern recapture (D-064). Exact discovery method, guardable access routes, interaction time, whether guards can temporarily block activation, and how to avoid invulnerable chokepoint camping remain unresolved |
| D-067 | Goblin tavern reclamation combines alternate routes and deception | **An occupied Secret Tavern has multiple viable infiltration approaches, including concealed passages and alternate routes, and the Goblin can use stealth, distraction and deception to lure defenders out of position.** These options work together so Dark Heroes guarding the master uprising mechanism (D-066) must anticipate different entry angles and misdirection, rather than winning by holding a single unavoidable chokepoint. This complements D-065's single master activation and D-064's denizen-assisted counterattack, without guaranteeing success. Exact passage topology, how routes are discovered, distraction abilities and AI responses, whether occupiers can close routes, and balance remain unapproved. This confirms option C, not route-only or deception-only play |
| D-068 | Goblin theft combines opportunistic steals and larger heists | **The Goblin Outlaw has two complementary personal theft loops:** quick, frequent, opportunistic theft from suitable distracted/vulnerable targets provides regular personal income, while **larger planned heists** require identifying a valuable target, scouting security or patrols, infiltrating, stealing and escaping for more substantial potential rewards. This uses the existing shared stealth/perception foundation (D-012 through D-014) and feeds the Goblin's personal gold progression (D-063), not the Light team's major-objective resource pool by default. Heists should offer meaningful higher stakes, not merely scale up the same pickpocket interaction. Specific victims, permitted theft sources, map locations, target selection, reward amounts, repeatability, alarm/heat rules, and possible team-objective overlaps are not yet approved |
| D-069 | Goblin theft produces local pursuit and cumulative area suspicion | **When a Goblin theft is witnessed or otherwise legitimately detected, nearby witnesses/guards respond with an immediate local pursuit.** Successfully escaping breaks pursuit according to ordinary sensory knowledge, rather than granting guards perfect hidden-target tracking. **Repeated detected theft incidents increase longer-term suspicion in that locality**, making subsequent theft attempts there riskier through more alert local defenders. Both immediate response and accumulated area-level suspicion are required. Suspicion represents discovered incidents and credible evidence, not a global Goblin bounty, automatic revelation of his position, or a team-wide Dark Hero alert. How incidents are identified, which NPCs react, thresholds, suspicion persistence/decay, investigation and whether players can reduce suspicion are unresolved; preserve D-012 through D-014 and D-068 |
| D-070 | Goblin area suspicion fades naturally and can be reduced actively | **Suspicion accumulated from detected Goblin theft in a locality (D-069) gradually decays when new credible incidents do not reinforce it.** The Goblin can also **take deliberate actions to accelerate the reduction**, such as bribery, deception, or removing evidence; these are example methods, not a finalized ability list. This concerns longer-term local vigilance and **does not instantly cancel an active chase, erase a witness's current sighting, or rewrite legitimately shared sighting evidence**. Both the passive recovery route and proactive mitigation are available, so active methods are not mandatory for suspicion to ever fall. Exact methods, targets, rates, costs, failure consequences, locality size and minimum suspicion remain unresolved. Keep standard perception boundaries D-012 through D-014 |
| D-071 | Goblin major heists include treasure and strategic Dark-operation raids | **The Goblin Outlaw can undertake different kinds of major planned heists:** **treasure heists** primarily deliver valuable personal gold/loot for his own progression, while **strategic heists** target Dark operations and yield benefits to the Light team by disrupting enemy activity. This preserves the Goblin's self-interested thief identity while giving him a meaningful team-support choice. Strategic heists do not automatically grant personal gold as well as shared resources, nor automatically erase banked Dark Energy. Any strategic interruption of corruption production must follow D-019's rule: it can affect only future passive accumulation, never reduce the existing Dark Energy total. The specific heist targets, Dark-operation effects, duration, economic payouts, available locations, repeatability, owner/team rewards and enemy counterplay remain unapproved. Preserve D-063's separate personal gold and shared objective accounts, and D-068's small theft versus major heist distinction |
| D-072 | Goblin strategic heists primarily target Dark logistics | **The Goblin Outlaw's primary strategic-heist specialization is stealing from or disrupting Dark logistics**, such as supply stores, deliveries, equipment and related operational assets. These raids should meaningfully weaken the Dark forces' ability to supply, equip or sustain their armies, while preserving the distinct treasure-heist path for personal wealth (D-071). This is a **primary focus, not an exclusive ban** on later corruption-related or intelligence-gathering heists. No specific inventory, convoy mechanic, army penalty, permanent supply denial, stealing enemy abilities, or payout is automatically approved. Exact target types, whether Dark armies use an explicit supply model, disruption consequences, duration, counterplay, mission visibility and whether the Goblin gains personal loot as well remain unresolved. Keep D-063's personal/shared accounting and D-019's rule against subtracting banked Dark Energy |
| D-073 | Hybrid Dark logistics: strategic infrastructure without minion-by-minion upkeep | **Major Dark supply stores and shipments matter as operational infrastructure**, creating meaningful points for Dark Heroes to protect and the Goblin to raid (D-072). **Individual Dark minions do not require constant supply or ration tracking** or continuous per-unit resupply micromanagement. Disrupting major stores or deliveries can affect **specific Dark army capabilities or reinforcement sources**, but the exact dependency and effects are not yet set. This is a hybrid logistics model, not a full per-unit supply simulation and not merely cosmetic supply props. No specific stockpile meter, consumption formula, reinforcement delay, debuff, loot transfer, permanent loss or Dark Energy change is approved. Protect D-019's restriction on already banked Dark Energy and D-063's distinct personal/shared resource accounts |
| D-074 | Target-dependent effects from successful Goblin logistics heists | **The operational consequence of a successful Goblin logistics raid depends on which Dark supply asset he attacks**, rather than every raid applying the same generic penalty: **supply depots affect their supported reinforcement operations; equipment stores affect access to their supported specialized equipment or capabilities; intercepted shipments affect the operations or destination those shipments were meant to supply**. A raid's impact must be traceable to its target and its actual operational dependency, enabling meaningful scouting and heist selection. Do not assume every depot affects every reinforcement, that all specialized abilities are globally disabled, or that every shipment affects every Dark Hero. Exact effects, duration, magnitude, recovery/resupply, stacking, whether any target has multiple dependencies, and alert/counterplay rules remain open. Preserve hybrid strategic logistics without per-minion supply micro (D-073), Goblin personal/shared economy (D-063), and no removal of banked Dark Energy (D-019) |
| D-075 | Hybrid recovery from Dark logistics disruption | **Operations disrupted by Goblin logistics raids gradually recover through ordinary resupply or replacement without requiring constant Dark Hero input.** Dark Heroes may **actively accelerate that recovery** by committing relevant resources, securing replacement shipments, or arranging repairs. This applies to the particular operation affected by the raided supply asset (D-074), not an automatic universal army recovery. Dark players must have a meaningful choice between recovering faster and pursuing other priorities, without making active intervention mandatory to ever regain capability. **Open:** passive recovery curves, resource/command costs, repair actions and targets, whether contested shipments can be intercepted again, effect stacking, exact acceleration and visibility. Does not create per-minion supply upkeep (D-073) or deduct banked Dark Energy (D-019) |
| D-076 | Alchemist recipe discovery through research-informed experimentation | **The Alchemist learns ingredient properties and recipe clues through exploration and research, then tests hypotheses by combining ingredients to discover potion recipes.** Research informs experimentation rather than automatically granting completed formulas, and experimentation should be intelligible rather than indiscriminate brute-force trial of arbitrary combinations. This preserves the GDD's biome-specific foraging, portable brewing, and recipe journal. The exact clues, property vocabulary, experimentation UI, how combinations resolve, journal detail, failure costs/side effects, formula permanence and whether original GDD per-match recipe randomization is retained are unresolved. Any retained randomization must be seeded and deterministic under authoritative simulation |
| D-077 | Alchemist failed experiments can waste ingredients or yield unintended concoctions | **An unsuccessful Alchemist experiment has more than one possible consequence:** some ingredient combinations produce no usable potion and consume the ingredients, while other combinations produce **unstable or unintended concoctions** that may still be usable, including surprising beneficial or risky effects. A failed-to-make-the-intended-potion result need not be a complete loss; experimentation should provide intelligible feedback that helps players learn ingredient properties rather than arbitrary punishment. Exact outcome probabilities or deterministic rules, what 'unstable' means, hazard effects, resource consumption/return, safe testing, inventory and journal treatment remain unapproved. Preserve D-076's research-informed discovery and server-authoritative determinism for any randomized outcomes |
| D-078 | Alchemist journal automatically records evidence and supports player hypotheses | **The Alchemist's journal automatically records observed ingredient properties, exploration/research clues, confirmed potion recipes and learnable findings from experiments**, minimizing manual bookkeeping. **Players can also actively connect clues and form tentative hypotheses in the journal** to guide later ingredient experiments. Player hypotheses must remain distinguishable from observed evidence and proven recipes; writing a plausible hypothesis does not automatically unlock or verify a formula. The journal is a reasoning tool, not merely a known-recipe list and not mandatory manual note transcription. Exact clue-linking interactions, journal layout, how hypotheses are stored/checked/invalidated, information sharing, misbrew records, and persistence across matches remain open. Preserve D-076 research-informed discovery and D-077 informative failures |
| D-079 | Alchemy combines per-match recipe discovery with stable underlying principles | **Alchemy's foundational interaction rules and learnable reasoning principles persist between matches, while meaningful potion recipes/combinations and some outcomes vary from match to match.** This intentionally synthesizes randomized-per-match discoveries (option B) with consistent underlying logic and variable results (option C), so players must investigate each run but can apply genuine knowledge from previous matches. Changed recipes must remain explainable through in-match ingredient clues, research and experimentation (D-076 through D-078), **not arbitrary untelegraphed rerolls of potion effects**. Any per-match variation must be initialized from an authoritative seed and yield deterministic simulation results. **Unresolved:** exactly which ingredient traits are stable versus variable; whether variation acts through ingredient affinities, catalysts, conditions or recipe associations; scope of potency/side-effect changes; cross-match journal memory; recipe balance; and whether players can predict outcomes from properties without brute-force testing. Do not lock in a specific algorithm or numeric parameters until chosen |
| D-080 | Alchemist ingredient affinities are substantially reshuffled each match | **The underlying alchemical interaction rules stay consistent between matches, but ingredient properties/affinities are redistributed substantially for each match.** Players must rediscover which properties the current run's ingredients have and infer compatible recipes using exploration clues, research, experiments and journal hypotheses (D-076 through D-079). Remembered formulas from past runs cannot be relied upon; acquired understanding of property interactions remains valuable. This selects **substantial per-match redistribution (option B)**, not the previously proposed option A where each ingredient keeps recognizable fixed core properties with only secondary traits shifting, and not option C where ingredient properties are fixed but only catalysts/conditions differ. **Do not presume** unrestricted uniform shuffling, specific properties, biome restrictions, guaranteed recipe counts, or fixed catalysts. Exact generation constraints, discoverability, stability of names/appearance/biome spawns, distribution balance and journal cross-match state remain unresolved. Generate any random variation with an authoritative seed, preserving deterministic results |
| D-081 | Progressive randomized Alchemist recipe accessibility across biomes | **Per-match ingredient affinity generation remains substantially variable (D-080), but is constrained to support a playable discovery progression:** ingredients available early in a match must permit **some accessible, useful combinations**, while rarer ingredients and more distant or dangerous biomes create opportunities for **more advanced discoveries**. The exact valid ingredient pairings and potion recipes still change between matches under stable, learnable alchemical principles (D-079). This is **progressive randomized discovery (option C)**, not unrestricted random assignment that could strand the Alchemist without early useful recipes, and not merely a flat balanced spread with no exploration progression. These are playability and progression constraints, not fixed early recipes, guaranteed specific potion effects, mandatory hard biome tiers, fixed recipe counts, or numeric probabilities. Discovery must remain clue-driven (D-076/D-078), seeded, deterministic and authoritative. **Open:** early-access definition, viable-usefulness criteria, biome/rarity distributions, advancement pace, algorithmic constraints and verification tests |
| D-082 | Hands-on potion discovery with streamlined repeat brewing | **The Alchemist's initial investigation and discovery of a potion involve active, hands-on brewing**, in which the player manipulates the brewing process and experiments with ingredient combinations rather than merely selecting inputs and receiving an outcome. **Once a recipe has been mastered, the Alchemist can reproduce it quickly without replaying the full discovery interaction**. This keeps the skill and discovery process meaningful while preventing routine potion preparation, particularly under time pressure, from becoming repetitive. It does not eliminate legitimate ingredients, costs, field-brewing constraints (D-045), or the need to rediscover match-specific recipes and affinities (D-079 through D-081). **Open:** physical brewing verbs and controls, exactly what qualifies as a mastered recipe, whether repeat brewing has a minimal interaction/time/cost, interruption/exposure rules, advanced laboratory requirements, whether a misbrew can be mastered, and combat-time restrictions. Preserve the distinction between unverified hypotheses and proven recipes (D-078) |
| D-083 | Alchemist reaction-control techniques unlock through character progression and add potion effects | **Hands-on brewing focuses on controlling an evolving reaction (option B),** rather than requiring a separate physical ingredient-preparation minigame. **Reaction-control techniques are unlocked progressively as the Alchemist advances; each newly unlocked technique gives the player a new way to influence a brew and, when used, adds an additional effect to the resulting potion.** This is a modular expansion of what a potion can do, not merely greater success chance or potency. Brewing should begin accessible with a workable basic interaction, and progression should grow the available technique/effect possibilities. Previously mastered potions remain quick to reproduce under D-082; how technique-enhanced formulas are mastered and reproduced is still open. **Unresolved:** which techniques are unlocked, progression prerequisites and costs, whether/which techniques can be combined per potion, effect limits/stacking, risk and tradeoffs, control input UX, reaction feedback, how mastery records technique variants, and interactions with run-randomized ingredients (D-079 through D-081). Examples like heat, stirring and timing are design possibilities, not yet mandatory fixed techniques |
| D-084 | Large seamless interconnected kingdom as target world architecture | **The target world for Grimm Dominion is a large, continuous, interconnected explorable kingdom, not an expanded small isolated arena or separate disconnected match maps.** Roads, settlements, wilderness, biomes, shrines, resources, Dark logistics, and the central Dark Lord castle must exist within the same coherent strategic geography, with distance, travel routes, concealment, territorial presence and competing objectives influencing the 4v4 match. This is an **end-state design and architecture constraint**, **not** a fixed world area, terrain-generator choice, commitment to a fixed layout, completed feature, or approval to bypass M1/M2 playtest gates. The M1 forest crossing is a reusable proven encounter in that future world, not the scale target. World representation, streaming/LOD, regional navigation and efficient server simulation must be designed to support large-scale continuity while preserving authoritative seeded simulation, per-viewer hidden information, and legitimate line-of-sight behavior. **Open:** macro geography, world variation, exact scale and travel pacing, landmark placement and route-count guarantees, whether there are hard natural barriers, height/cave/interior transitions, match length, performance budgets and when specific world systems enter implementation |
| D-085 | Guided procedural kingdom geography with fixed strategic constraints | **The huge seamless kingdom uses guided procedural generation (choice C):** match seeds can produce substantially different arrangements of biomes, settlements, roads, wilderness, landmarks and travel routes, but generation is constrained by designed strategic relationships, discoverability and playability tests. This is **not** a largely fixed authored map with only cosmetic variation (A), nor unconstrained generation that may strand factions, block progression, or create unfair chokepoints (B). Preserve one traversable kingdom (D-084), castle and Dark Lord objective (D-015 through D-018), class-specific opportunities, meaningful competing Dark defenses, fair resource access, multiple feasible approaches where intended, coherent logistics, and sight/cover rules. All generated choices must be seeded and deterministic under authoritative simulation, and validation should reject/retry unplayable results without changing approved mechanics. **Open:** topology (castle placement, regional hubs, outer routes), which geography elements may move, specific generator stages and constraints, world size/travel time, how much geography is revealed, and test thresholds. Do not approve a fixed radial layout, castle-at-geometric-centre rule, specific biome ordering, or implementation algorithm by implication |
| D-086 | Hybrid castle approaches and independent regional route networks | **The kingdom uses a hybrid strategic network (choice C):** the Dark Lord's castle anchors several meaningful major approaches, while settlements and regional hubs are connected by outer roads, wilderness corridors, and alternate routes that form independent connections and loops. This allows Dark Heroes to prioritize defensible roads and logistics while Light Heroes have opportunities to travel, evade, flank, and pursue objectives without all traffic being forced through the castle. **The castle is the central victory objective, not necessarily the geometric center of the terrain.** This is a **procedural topology constraint** under D-085, not a fixed world drawing or named set of roads. It rejects a compulsory radial-only funnel (option A) and an entirely unconstrained distributed network with no strategic castle approach (option B). The generator must validate continuous traversability, alternative approaches where intended, meaningful chokepoints with counterplay, seeded determinism and legal sight/cover behavior. **Unresolved:** route counts and grades, road speed and transportation, specific placement of castle and hubs, strategic distance/travel pacing, rivers and passes, discoverability, map visibility, patrol coverage and node/edge-generation algorithms |
## Provisional engineering direction (requires Fable technical validation)
- Browser-based Three.js + TypeScript renderer/client and Vite toolchain.
- An authoritative Node/TypeScript simulation and multiplayer server, potentially Colyseus (check current 0.18+ docs and actual installed version).
- Client-only React for out-of-game menus/HUD *if justified*; do not introduce it before proving a need.
- GLB/glTF 3D asset pipeline; original or licensed meshes and textures.
- Fixed-timestep simulation, validated commands, and strict per-team information projection.
- Bots and humans submit compatible commands. Bot difficulty and takeover semantics are not yet decided.
- A narrow initial playable test may use a subset of the eight slots, but the full product remains 4v4.

## Unresolved rules (do NOT silently decide these as canon)
1. **Core rule decided by D-015 to D-018:** Dark Lord starts dormant; Light can breach sanctum and kill him before awakening; Dark Energy accrues naturally over time, receives small one-time gains from evil deeds by Dark Heroes and minions, and accrues faster through corruption sources; awakening triggers Dark Lord + Dark Heroes vs Light Heroes final battle. **D-019/D-020 confirm** Light Heroes can slow awakening by sabotaging corruption sources, without directly reversing accumulated Dark Energy. **Unresolved:** meter formula/tiers, qualifying evil deeds, corruption/constructible source types, placement rules, source stacking and maintenance, boss abilities, pre-awakening sanctum vulnerability, post-awakening terminal rules, and whether sleeping influence exists.
2. What camera(s) and controls does each Light Hero have? What triggers switching? How is aiming balanced against high-level RTS control?
3. How does the Dark Hero switch between physical action and macro-command? Does game time continue while commanding?
4. What constitutes Dark victory if Light Heroes die or the awakened boss wins? **D-032 confirms body-and-ghost revival routes**, but resurrection costs/limits, body interactions, ghost capabilities, full-team defeat, disconnects and timeout behavior are unresolved. Awakening alone **does not** grant Dark victory.
5. Target completed match duration, spawn rules, and other team coordination mechanics. **Confirmed sightings** are shared as alerts; **live Scout vision** remains owner-private per D-012. Other communication and evidence-sharing rules are still open.
6. Exact bot replacement policy: immediately upon empty slot, after disconnection delay, or only at match start.
7. Launch format and deployment hosting details; accounts, identity, payments, and moderation later.
8. Which character and encounter are chosen for the first vertical increment. Goblin + one Dark Commander are a **proposal**, not confirmed.

## Rejected or superseded
- Earlier D-017 wording that **every evil deed increases the passive awakening rate** is expressly replaced by D-017 (instant small reward) and D-020 (rate increase from corruption sources). Do not recreate that obsolete mechanic.
- Godot as the main game platform.
- A 2D-first prototype that postpones the Warcraft-inspired 3D foundation.
- A one-versus-four final game structure, superseded by explicit 4v4.
- Treating mobile as the immediate first platform, superseded by PC browser first.
- Bots as development-only tools, superseded by permanent bot support.
- Mock scenes, screenshots, or untested code reported as a playable milestone.
